# Load test, 12 Nov 2026

Server: VPS with 6 vCPU and 11 GB RAM, shared with other services. Baseline before the test: load 0.26, BRouter 227 MB of its 2 GB limit.
Third-party services (Garmin, Google, tile servers) were not load-tested.

Concurrency, 41 km legs:

| Concurrent | Throughput | p50 | Errors |
|---|---|---|---|

8 km legs: 24–39 legs/s at 8–24 concurrent, no errors.

Peak during the test: BRouter about one core and 527 MB, server load 2.7.

**Findings and changes**
- Above the thread count BRouter cancels the oldest requests ("killed by thread-priority-watchdog"). Threads raised from 4 to 6 (one per core); the app now retries a cancelled leg up to 3 times with a short random pause.
- A leg longer than about 1000 km does not finish in BRouter's 60 s. The app now says so and suggests adding a point in between. Typical routes with points every few dozen km are unaffected.

## Mountain passes file (static)

`passes.geojson`, 9.6 MB, served zstd-compressed as 1.7 MB. 30 downloads, 10 at a time: 1.9 s in total, p50 0.48 s, Caddy CPU under 10%.

The storage quota check summed the size of all of a user's documents on every save, reading their bodies. Document size is now a column, with an index on (user, updated). The 50 MB quota answers 413 as intended. Test data was removed and the database vacuumed.

## Capacity, roughly

One person editing a route sends one or two legs per change. With 6 legs in parallel at 2–3 legs/s for 40 km legs and much more for short ones, the server handles dozens of people editing at the same time. Long single legs (hundreds of km) are the expensive case.

## After the test

- Road legs are limited to 100 km in a straight line between their two points: the app refuses longer legs with a hint, and routing now goes through maps-api, which checks the leg before passing it to BRouter (BRouter is no longer reachable directly). Straight legs are not limited; their terrain samples are capped at 2000 per leg.

## Not done

- No rate limit per IP on the routing endpoint; with legs capped at 100 km one client can still keep the router busy with many short requests.
- `passes.geojson` has no Cache-Control; browsers revalidate it by ETag each time.
